Farm hazard decision scan

Turn observable farm conditions into a clear hazard–harm–control–report chain without inventing a local risk score.

Fictional scenario

Overnight rain changed the training-farm entry

No work is under way. From outside the marked training area, a learner can see a fresh rut across the east lane, a portable pedestrian barrier no longer covering its marked position, and an unfamiliar parked machine partly narrowing the signed walking route. The current local risk matrix and reporting channel are deliberately not supplied.

Use this boundary: Treat every detail as fictional. Inspect and reason; do not enter, move equipment or recreate the scene.

Observable evidence

Shared space

Interactive practice

Complete the job

Classify the evidence

Choose the most useful description for each observation.

Fresh rut after rain

Barrier away from its marked position

Machine narrowing the walking route

Support: Use precise nouns: condition, exposure, control, authority and missing information.

Choose the strongest decisions

Use the supplied evidence only.

What is the strongest first response?

Support: Ask: what can the learner safely do before the local decision is made?

Which proposed response best reflects hierarchy-aligned reasoning?

Support: Trace the path between the hazard and the person, then ask what could interrupt it.

Which evidence would best show that a confirmed control needs review?

Support: Review is triggered by evidence that assumptions or controls no longer match the scene.

Build the decision chain

Use fictional, de-identified wording.

Support: Do not enter real names, emergency contacts or a local risk score.

Quality check

What a strong practice record shows

Success criteria

  • Describes observable evidence without blame
  • Separates hazard, possible harm and control
  • Keeps approval with the authorised person
  • Names a reporting and review point

Support

Use the scan path: people, plant, animals, environment, task interaction and changing conditions.

Extension

Compare a higher-level and lower-level control for one fictional exposure and explain what residual risk would still need monitoring.

Teacher delivery note

Set-up

Use the supplied fictional stimulus. Substitute local material only when authorised, privacy-safe and free of controlled assessment content.

Observe

Listen for evidence, source choice, uncertainty and authority boundaries—not just the selected answer.

Debrief

Ask one learner to defend a decision, one to identify an assumption and one to name the next authorised source or person.

Boundary: The local risk matrix, emergency plan and site controls remain teacher/workplace controlled. Completion creates a private supplementary-learning record only; it does not authorise work, replace workplace records, submit evidence to the RTO or determine competency.
