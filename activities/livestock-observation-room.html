Fictional scenario

A fictional group approaches a supervised yard transfer

Before movement, the group is standing together near the shaded side of the yard. One animal repeatedly turns its head toward a loose sheet of material moving in the wind. During the teacher-controlled rehearsal, the group spreads and the same animal increases its pace. The supervisor pauses the rehearsal. No animal is handled through this page.

Use this boundary: Species-specific movement, position, facility use, restraint and intervention are controlled by the teacher's current plan and direct supervision.

Interactive practice

Complete the job

Observation or interpretation?

Classify the wording.

The animal turned its head toward the moving sheet four times in one minute.

The animal was being difficult on purpose.

The group spread from one cluster into two smaller groups.

The animal definitely has a health condition.

Support: Use verbs that another observer could see, hear, count or locate.

Allocate communication roles

Match the role to the field-level responsibility.

Authorised supervisor

Nominated observer

Record keeper

Support: Roles should make communication faster and responsibility clearer, not spread authority equally.

Respond to changed behaviour

The supervisor has already paused the rehearsal.

What should the learner contribute next?

Support: Use a closed-loop brief: observation, location/time, acknowledgement and confirmed direction.

Complete the observation, role and handover record

Use only the supplied fictional facts.

Support: Do not enter a diagnosis, treatment or handling technique.

Quality check

What a strong practice record shows

Success criteria

  • Uses objective animal and environment observations
  • Separates evidence from diagnosis or motive
  • Allocates roles under authorised supervision
  • Produces a concise before/during/after handover

Support

Use the observation frame: animal/group, environment, change over time, action directed, outcome.

Extension

Rewrite one interpretation as two observable sentences and one question for the supervisor.

Observe

Listen for evidence, source choice, uncertainty and authority boundaries—not just the selected answer.

Debrief

Ask one learner to defend a decision, one to identify an assumption and one to name the next authorised source or person.
