Fictional scenario

A fictional forecast affects an outdoor work plan

At 8:00 am on a fictional training day, local observations show increasing cloud and a freshening wind. A saved forecast issued at 6:00 am mentions possible afternoon thunderstorms. A fictional warning issued at 8:20 am covers the training district from midday. The planned afternoon task is an outdoor material transfer. No real weather decision should use these details.

Use this boundary: Real decisions require current Bureau of Meteorology information and the local emergency or contingency plan.

Quality check

What a strong practice record shows

Success criteria

  • Checks authority, timestamp, location and relevance
  • Combines local observation with official information
  • Handles uncertainty without ignoring risk
  • Uses the authorised contingency path

Teacher delivery note

Set-up

Use the supplied fictional stimulus. Substitute local material only when authorised, privacy-safe and free of controlled assessment content.

Observe

Listen for evidence, source choice, uncertainty and authority boundaries—not just the selected answer.

Debrief

Ask one learner to defend a decision, one to identify an assumption and one to name the next authorised source or person.

Boundary: Real decisions require current Bureau of Meteorology information and the local emergency/contingency plan. Completion creates a private supplementary-learning record only; it does not authorise work, replace workplace records, submit evidence to the RTO or determine competency.
